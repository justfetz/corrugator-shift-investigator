# Asking questions

A question is read against the scope in the left rail, answered by the offline planner in tens of milliseconds, and given an address. This page lists what it understands, what it refuses and how to read what comes back. [The workbench, step by step](WORKBENCH.md) walks the page itself; [the URL grammar](URLS.md) is the full reference for addresses.

## The scope comes first

The rail's three controls are part of every question:

| Control | Values | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Production day | Monday to Friday, the window the page footer names (from 1 January 2025 to the day before the site was last deployed) | the anchor; a day runs 07:00 to 07:00 and is labeled by its starting date |
| Shift | 1 (07–15), 2 (15–23), 3 (23–07), All | one 480-minute shift, or All for the whole production day and comparisons between shifts |
| Window | Day, Week, Month, 4-4-5 | the window a question that names none is asked over: the day, its Monday-to-Friday week, its calendar month, or its 4-4-5 accounting period |

A question that names no day or window reads the selected day, every quantity and the downtime review alike. A question that asks for a number and names no shift answers all shifts, counts, minutes, MSF, percentages and speed alike, whatever the shift control says and with or without an ask word ("how much downtime this week" reads what "total downtime this week" reads), while the shift control still scopes the investigations (why, what caused or drove, what went wrong) and any question that says "this shift".

That is the owner's scope ruling of 13 November 2026. One reading of it is this router's own, pending the owner's word: a production day's per-shift answers (its speed, its quality, its downtime by place or kind) keep the shift control, because their all-shift form today is the three shifts compared, led by the slowest shift, which is another question. A shift or window the question names wins over the controls, and the address says so: with shift 2 selected, "Why was shift 1 slow?" answers shift 1 (`/corr/1/day/2026-09-14/shift/1?measure=overview`), "this week" under Day asks about the week, and "How much downtime on 1st shift last week?" answers the week before for shift 1 (`/corr/1/week/2026-09-07/shift/1?measure=pareto`). A comparison still needs its scope: with one shift selected, "Compare that with first shift" is refused ("Selected shift only") rather than quietly querying another shift, so choose **All** first. [The URL grammar](URLS.md#the-questions-own-scope-issue-105) has the rules. A month named without a year is the latest month of that name on or before the selected day: "How did December go?" asked on 14 September 2026 is December 2025.

## What is refused, and why

A refusal says what to ask instead; it never guesses.

- **No measurable goal.** "How can we be better?", "Adjust the knife settings to cut trim" and "How much will we save if we fix the splicer?" are refused: the records hold no intervention outcomes, no machine state and no costs. Choose an objective the records can answer: fewer downtime minutes, less recorded waste, or better grade-target attainment in the selected scope.
- **Today, and a day with nothing to measure.** "Yesterday" is the production day before the selected one ("Why was yesterday slow?" on Monday 14 September answers Friday 11 September), but "How did today go?" asks you to use the day control, and "What happened yesterday?" is refused like any question that names nothing to measure.
- **A count the records do not hold.** "How many rolls did we use?", "How many boards did we lose at the stacker?", "How many step-ups?", "How many splices did we run?", "How many orders?", "How many hours of downtime?", "How many reject sheets?", "What was our minutes per setup?", "Feet per stop this week", "Cost per setup", "Sheets per minute", "Square feet per minute", "How long did setups take?", "Average setup length", "How long was the longest stop?", "Average stops this week", "How long were we slow?" and "How much downtime in hours?" are refused with what can be counted: paper is recorded in lineal feet, MSF and paper runs, rejects in square feet by reason, a missed splice as a stop reason, downtime in minutes. A question that names a count or a unit the router cannot place ("How did we do on glue this week?", "How many widgets on shift 2?") is refused the same way, never answered with the KPI summary or the overview (issue #113). A number asked with no shift named is answered for every shift together (the scope rule above); say "this shift" or name the shift for one shift's. A quantity asked of two shifts side by side ("how much scrap on shift 1 versus shift 2", "compare shifts on downtime minutes") is refused rather than answered with the shift comparison, which opens with speed, and so is a quantity of a ranked shift ("how many minutes of downtime did the slowest shift have"): the router does not rank shifts while it reads a question. Stops or minutes of each place, reason or kind ("how many minutes of downtime at each place this week", "stops by kind this week") are the Pareto grouped by it, in the unit asked, and a breakdown asked in a unit ("waste breakdown in percent") is the breakdown. An average stop by reason is in the downtime review, beside each reason's minutes and stops.
- **Every word is read, or the question is refused.** One quantity, minutes per stop, each production day's downtime and each production day's stops answer only a question whose every word the router consumes: the ask words, the measure and its unit, the stop reason, place, kind, waste line or grade it filters by (with the connector before it: "caused by the stacker", "due to order changes", "by operators"), the window, a window compared with the one before, the shift, a kept ratio's "per setup" or "per stop" and the average it is, and a closed table of function words ("how", "many", "did", "we", "the", "this", "total", "line" and the like; `agent.FUNCTION_WORDS`, the same table in `evals/run.py`). Any other word asks a different question, so the router refuses it, naming what it asks for and at most three questions that answer: a ranking ("which shift had the most setups", "what was the fewest stops in a day"), a grouping or a rate ("stops by type", "stop rate", "stop distribution", "ratio of stops to setups"), a statistic ("median stop length", "90th percentile stop length"), a subset ("how many long stops", "stops under 2 minutes", "brief stops", "stops before noon", "stops during setups"), a share ("what proportion of stops were at the stacker"), a filter the records do not name ("stops at the slitter", "planned stops", "minutes of planned downtime", "minutes per stop for crew A", "stops at the wet end"), two quantities ("setups and stops"), one stop rather than a window's ("how long did the last stop last"), a shift named in words the router does not read ("shift one", "the last shift": name it by its number), a window it does not apply ("the previous day", "the week before last", "the same day last week", "month to date"), or a rolling window ("the last 7 days", "the past week": the windows are the production day or yesterday, the week, the month and the 4-4-5 period). No list of such words could be complete, so the rule is the other way round: only the words the route reads pass. What an answer already carries stays answered: "compare stops this week to last week" and "how many stops this week versus last week" are the count with the week before beside it, "average stop length", "how long did our stops last on average" and "how much downtime did we have per stop" are minutes per stop, "average run length" feet per setup, "how long did knife jams take" that reason's minutes, "how many stops were order changes" the stops with that reason (stops said beat any other count noun), "how many sheets at the knife" every sheet cut, "feet a minute" the speed. "A day" and "daily" read as "per day", and of stops or downtime "each day", "every day" and "by day" do too: "stops each day" and "how many stops a day" are the stops of each production day, "downtime by day", "daily downtime" and "each day's downtime" each day's downtime, "daily setups" a ratio the records do not keep (refused with three questions, as every refusal here is); "setups each day" and "MSF by day" stay the groupings they are, never the per-day mean. Minutes per stop, each day's downtime and each day's stops are kept for all stops together, so asked of one reason, place or kind ("stacker minutes per stop", "maintenance downtime per day", "stops per day at the stacker") they are refused, never answered with every stop's figure, and so is an average across a window's days ("average stops per day"), never the busiest day. A question that asks why, what caused, drove or is behind something, or where it comes from ("why was downtime high every day this week", "what is causing the stops this week", "where is the downtime coming from") is an investigation, never a quantity: the downtime review or the Pareto by place, on the shift control.
- **An ambiguous window.** A bare "Compare last week" asks which two windows to compare, for example "this week compared with last week".
- **A per-shift view over a period.** The setup matrix, orders, notes and rankings are per shift and per day; under Week, Month or 4-4-5 they ask you to switch Window back to Day.
- **A window outside the data.** A weekend, or any window before the year's first day or after its last, is unavailable with its reason, never zero. A comparison is withheld unless both windows are complete.

## Reading an answer

Read an answer as **recorded finding → question to investigate → something a person verifies**. A large knife-jam total says where to look with the crew; it does not prove a worn part or justify changing machine settings. Notes describe reported observations. A target gap is not guaranteed recoverable output, and this dataset cannot establish savings, profitability or the best plant-wide improvement.

Every figure names the query and row it came from: the `(query N · row M)` link beside a finding opens the query box at that step, with its SQL and bound values, and "What came back, per query" at the foot of the card holds each step's full result. "Complete" means the investigation finished, not that it proved a cause. If an answer misses your intent, narrow the question and read the queries it ran.

## The money on every card

Every card shows its window's money, priced at the rates in force: a dollar line under the headline (`≈ $7,762 recorded losses this shift · $5.81M/yr at this rate`, a chip per bucket), the levers ranked by dollars a year under the charts (hover a bar for its formula; Table shows the same rows), dollars on each tile that measures a loss, and the arithmetic at the bottom of the card. The rates are assumptions, the owner's post by default and never a plant's: **Your rates** in the rail shows each with its source, **Change rates** edits them and re-prices the whole page in the browser with no request, and the address then carries them as `rates=`, so the link reproduces the same figures. **Show as** switches tiles and bars between minutes, percent and dollars. A contribution margin is not set until you set one: until then no lost contribution is priced, never as $0. [The cost model](COST_MODEL.md#on-the-card-issue-31b) has every formula.

## Every answer is an address

An offline answer's address goes into the address bar as it lands: the path is the scope (`/corr/<machine>/<grain>/<day>[/shift/<n>]`) and the query is the question (`measure=` and its modifiers). Back and forward reopen earlier cards, a bookmark or a pasted link reproduces the answer in any browser, and adding `.json` to the path or `format=pdf` to the query gives the same answer as data or as a PDF. A typed question is a shortcut: `GET /q?day=…&shift=…&q=…` redirects to the one canonical address for it. [The URL grammar](URLS.md) has every measure, modifier and alias.

## Asking the analyst

**Ask the analyst** on an answer, or beside the ask bar, sends the question to a model once: it plans the queries and writes an interpretation whose every number is filled in from a query row. It appears only when the site has a model configured, it takes seconds instead of milliseconds, and its answer has no address, since a model's choices are not reproducible from a URL. [The analyst](ANALYST.md) says what it may and may not do.
